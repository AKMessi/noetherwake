## Intuition

Accumulating a continuous rate and then asking how fast the accumulated amount changes returns the original rate. Differentiation and integration undo each other under the right conditions.

## Formalism

$$
\frac{d}{dx}\int_a^x f(t)\,dt=f(x),\qquad\int_a^b F\prime=F(b)-F(a)
$$

Adding a short interval contributes approximately f(x) times its width. Divide by that width and shrink it; continuity forces the average height toward f(x).

[Explore integral](concept:integral). [Explore continuity](concept:continuity). [Explore derivative](concept:derivative).

## Worked example

F(x)=x^2. Evaluate the integral of F prime from 3 to 4.

Start with the defining relationship. Adding a short interval contributes approximately f(x) times its width. Divide by that width and shrink it; continuity forces the average height toward f(x). Substitute the given values: **`4^2-3^2` = 7 **. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

The first form needs continuity at the evaluation point; the evaluation formula requires a suitable antiderivative.

A common trap is: **confusing an antiderivative with a definite total**. A definite integral is an endpoint difference; the arbitrary constant cancels.
