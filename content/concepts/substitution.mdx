## Intuition

A change of variable renames the inner process and accounts for how its scale changes. It is the chain rule read backward.

## Formalism

$$
\int f(g(x))g\prime(x)\,dx=\int f(u)\,du
$$

Set u=g(x). Its differential du includes the inner rate g prime times dx, so both the integrand and interval measure transform together.

[Explore fundamental theorem](concept:fundamental-theorem). [Explore chain rule](concept:chain-rule).

## Worked example

Integrate 2x*(x^2+3) from 0 to 1.

Start with the defining relationship. Set u=g(x). Its differential du includes the inner rate g prime times dx, so both the integrand and interval measure transform together. Substitute the given values: **`3+0.5` = 3.5 **. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

For definite integrals, change the bounds too. A missing derivative factor makes a tempting substitution invalid.

A common trap is: **changing the integrand but keeping old bounds**. Label each bound with its variable; map both endpoints through u=g(x).
