## Intuition

In an ohmic element, a larger voltage difference drives proportionally more current. Resistance is the ratio between the two, summarizing the material and geometry under fixed conditions.

## Formalism

$$V=IR,\qquad P=VI=I^2R$$

Ohm law is an empirical constitutive relation. Each coulomb loses V joules and I coulombs pass per second, so dissipated power is VI.

[Explore voltage](concept:voltage). [Explore current](concept:current). [Explore ratios](concept:ratios).

## Worked example

A 3 ohm resistor has 4 V across it. Find current.

Start with the defining relationship. Ohm law is an empirical constitutive relation. Each coulomb loses V joules and I coulombs pass per second, so dissipated power is VI. Substitute the given values: **`4/3` = 1.3333 A**. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

Resistance may change with temperature; diodes and many other devices are not ohmic. Ohm law is not a universal conservation law.

A common trap is: **every device has constant v/i**. Plot current against voltage and check whether the slope stays fixed.
