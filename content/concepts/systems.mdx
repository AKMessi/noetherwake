## Intuition

Two equations constrain the same unknowns. Their common solution is where both stories are true at once. Elimination combines constraints so one unknown cancels.

## Formalism

$$x+y=s,\quad x-y=d\quad\Rightarrow\quad x=\frac{s+d}{2}$$

Add the equations: positive y and negative y cancel, leaving twice x. Recover y by substitution.

[Explore linear functions](concept:linear-functions).

## Worked example

Solve x+y=3+4, x-y=3-4. Enter x.

Start with the defining relationship. Add the equations: positive y and negative y cancel, leaving twice x. Recover y by substitution. Substitute the given values: **`3` = 3 **. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

Parallel distinct lines have no common solution; coincident lines have infinitely many. A numerical solver must detect both cases.

A common trap is: **combining only one side of the equations**. Add the entire left sides and entire right sides as equal quantities.
