## Intuition

A logarithm counts multiplicative growth steps. Because it undoes exponentiation, it turns products into sums and measures relative rather than absolute scale.

## Formalism

$$
\ln(ab)=\ln a+\ln b,\qquad(\ln x)\prime=1/x
$$

Differentiate exp(ln x)=x with the chain rule: exp(ln x) times the unknown derivative is 1. Since exp(ln x)=x, the rate is 1/x.

## Worked example

Find the derivative of ln(x) at x=3.

Start with the defining relationship. Differentiate exp(ln x)=x with the chain rule: exp(ln x) times the unknown derivative is 1. Since exp(ln x)=x, the rate is 1/x. Substitute the given values: **`1/3` = 0.33333 **. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

The real natural logarithm accepts only positive inputs. Logarithms do not distribute across addition.

A common trap is: **treating log of a sum as sum of logs**. Test ln(1+1) against ln(1)+ln(1); the right side is zero.
