## Intuition

The area of a rectangle changes when either side changes. Two thin strips contribute at first order; their tiny corner contributes only second-order area.

## Formalism

$$(uv)\prime=u\prime v+uv\prime$$

Expand (u+du)(v+dv)-uv. The terms v du and u dv survive division by the input step; du dv vanishes in the derivative limit.

[Explore derivative](concept:derivative). [Explore quadratics](concept:quadratics).

## Worked example

u(0)=3, u prime(0)=2, v(0)=4, v prime(0)=3. Find (uv) prime(0).

Start with the defining relationship. Expand (u+du)(v+dv)-uv. The terms v du and u dv survive division by the input step; du dv vanishes in the derivative limit. Substitute the given values: **`2*4+3*3` = 17 **. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

The derivative of a product is not the product of derivatives. This rule assumes both factors are differentiable.

A common trap is: **multiplying the two derivatives**. Sketch the two thin area strips; each retains one original side.
