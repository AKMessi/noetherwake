## Intuition

Each electrostatic source contributes a vector field. The field at a point is the vector sum of those contributions, so cancellation and reinforcement depend on direction.

## Formalism

$$
\mathbf E_{total}=\sum_i\mathbf E_i
$$

Electric force is linear in the source charges in this model. Divide the summed test-charge forces by the same probe charge to obtain a summed field.

[Explore electric field](concept:electric-field). [Explore systems](concept:systems). [Explore vectors](concept:vectors).

## Worked example

Fields at a point are 3 N/C right and 4 N/C left. Find net signed field.

Start with the defining relationship. Electric force is linear in the source charges in this model. Divide the summed test-charge forces by the same probe charge to obtain a summed field. Substitute the given values: **`3-4` = -1 N/C**. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

Add components, not magnitudes. Material response may require finding induced source charges before summing.

A common trap is: **adding field magnitudes when directions oppose**. Draw each source field arrow at the observation point and give components signs.
