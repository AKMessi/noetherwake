## Intuition

Momentum can remain conserved even when kinetic energy becomes heat or deformation. A perfectly inelastic collision joins the bodies, forcing a common final velocity.

## Formalism

$$v_f=\frac{m_1v_1+m_2v_2}{m_1+m_2}$$

Choose both colliding bodies as the system and neglect external impulse. Equate total momentum before and after; common final velocity factors out.

[Explore momentum](concept:momentum). [Explore kinetic energy](concept:kinetic-energy).

## Worked example

A 3 kg mass moving at 4 m/s sticks to a stationary 3 kg mass. Find final speed.

Start with the defining relationship. Choose both colliding bodies as the system and neglect external impulse. Equate total momentum before and after; common final velocity factors out. Substitute the given values: **`4/2` = 2 m/s**. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

Kinetic energy is conserved only in elastic collisions. Momentum conservation alone cannot determine both final velocities in a general collision.

A common trap is: **assuming every collision conserves kinetic energy**. Compute kinetic energy before and after the common-velocity solution; missing mechanical energy changes form.
