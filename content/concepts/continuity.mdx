## Intuition

Continuity means that nearby inputs produce outputs near the output at the point. The graph has no missing value or jump at that location.

## Formalism

$$
\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=f(a)
$$

Existence of the nearby limit and equality with the actual value are separate checks. Filling a removable hole with its limit restores continuity.

[Explore limits](concept:limits).

## Worked example

To make f continuous at x=3, set f(3) equal to the limit of 2x+4. What value is needed?

Start with the defining relationship. Existence of the nearby limit and equality with the actual value are separate checks. Filling a removable hole with its limit restores continuity. Substitute the given values: **`2*3+4` = 10 **. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

Continuity does not guarantee a derivative: an absolute-value corner is continuous but has conflicting one-sided slopes.

A common trap is: **assuming continuity guarantees smoothness**. Compare the left and right slopes at the corner of an absolute-value graph.
