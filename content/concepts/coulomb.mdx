## Intuition

A point charge influences space in every direction. In three dimensions, spherical area grows as radius squared, so a spherically spreading interaction weakens with the inverse square of distance.

## Formalism

$$
F=k_e\frac{|q_1q_2|}{r^2}
$$

Coulomb law is an empirical electrostatic law consistent with spherical symmetry and flux conservation. The signs determine attraction or repulsion; the magnitude follows the inverse-square scale.

## Worked example

Use normalized electrostatic constant k=1 N*m^2/C^2. Charges 3 C and 4 C are 2 m apart. Find force magnitude.

Start with the defining relationship. Coulomb law is an empirical electrostatic law consistent with spherical symmetry and flux conservation. The signs determine attraction or repulsion; the magnitude follows the inverse-square scale. Substitute the given values: **`3*4/4` = 3 N**. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

Use point charges or suitable spherical distributions at rest. Finite objects, materials and moving charges require additional modelling.

A common trap is: **doubling distance halves the force**. Compare the area of a sphere at r and 2r; it grows by four.
