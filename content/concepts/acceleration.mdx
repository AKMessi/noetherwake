## Intuition

Acceleration measures the rate of velocity change. It can change direction, speed, or both. A negative acceleration is a direction on the chosen axis, not automatically slowing down.

## Formalism

$$a=\frac{dv}{dt}=\frac{d^2x}{dt^2}$$

Apply the local-rate definition to velocity. Integrating acceleration reconstructs velocity up to its initial value.

[Explore velocity](concept:velocity). [Explore derivative](concept:derivative).

## Worked example

Velocity changes from 3 m/s to 4 m/s in 2 s. Find average acceleration.

Start with the defining relationship. Apply the local-rate definition to velocity. Integrating acceleration reconstructs velocity up to its initial value. Substitute the given values: **`(4-3)/2` = 0.5 m/s^2**. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

An object moving left can speed up with negative acceleration. Slowing depends on the relative signs of velocity and acceleration.

A common trap is: **negative acceleration always means slowing**. Compare the signs of velocity and acceleration: matching signs increase speed in one dimension.
