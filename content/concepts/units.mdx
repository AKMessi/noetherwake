## Intuition

A unit is part of a measurement, not an annotation added afterward. A rate divides one kind of quantity by another. Dimensional consistency lets you reject equations before calculating.

## Formalism

$$[v]=LT^{-1},\quad[a]=LT^{-2},\quad[F]=MLT^{-2}$$

Speed is displacement per elapsed time. Acceleration is the change in that speed per elapsed time, introducing a second time denominator.

[Explore ratios](concept:ratios). [Explore exponents](concept:exponents).

## Worked example

Convert 3 kilometres to metres.

Start with the defining relationship. Speed is displacement per elapsed time. Acceleration is the change in that speed per elapsed time, introducing a second time denominator. Substitute the given values: **`1000*3` = 3000 m**. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

Dimensions constrain a formula but cannot fix dimensionless constants or prove a physical law. Angles in radians are dimensionless ratios.

A common trap is: **treating a correct number as sufficient**. State what quantity is measured and derive its unit from the defining ratio.
