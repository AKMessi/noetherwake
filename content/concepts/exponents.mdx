## Intuition

A power records repeated multiplication, so it describes repeated scaling. Squaring a length scales an area; doubling a side multiplies the area by four.

## Formalism

$$
x^m x^n=x^{m+n}
$$

Count the factors of x in each group. Joining the groups adds factor counts; taking a power of a power multiplies those counts.

[Explore signed numbers](concept:signed-numbers). [Explore ratios](concept:ratios).

## Worked example

Evaluate 2^3.

Start with the defining relationship. Count the factors of x in each group. Joining the groups adds factor counts; taking a power of a power multiplies those counts. Substitute the given values: **`2^3` = 8 **. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

Fractional powers of negative real numbers require domain care. A negative exponent is a reciprocal, not a negative result.

A common trap is: **confusing powers of powers with sums**. Expand two small powers into repeated factors before using the rule.
