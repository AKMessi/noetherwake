## Intuition

Walking three metres east and four metres north does not put you seven metres from where you started. The two movements point in different directions. Their combined displacement is the diagonal of a right triangle.

A vector stores both **magnitude and direction**. Components describe that vector relative to chosen axes. Rotate the axes and the components change; the physical displacement stays the same. Before adding forces or velocities, agree on the coordinate system.

## Formalism

In perpendicular Cartesian axes, write a two-dimensional vector as $\mathbf{u}=(u_x,u_y)$. Add matching components and compute length with Pythagoras:

$$\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}=(u_x+v_x,u_y+v_y),\qquad |\mathbf{u}|=\sqrt{u_x^2+u_y^2}.$$

Multiplying by a positive scalar stretches a vector; a negative scalar also reverses its direction. A unit vector has length one and isolates direction. For nonzero $\mathbf{u}$, its unit vector is $\hat{\mathbf{u}}=\mathbf{u}/|\mathbf{u}|$.

Use [coordinates](concept:coordinates) to locate the components and [trigonometry](concept:trig) when an angle and magnitude are given instead.

## Worked example

A drone travels $3\,\mathrm{m}$ east, then $4\,\mathrm{m}$ north. Choose east as positive $x$ and north as positive $y$.

$$\Delta\mathbf{r}=(3,0)\,\mathrm{m}+(0,4)\,\mathrm{m}=(3,4)\,\mathrm{m}.$$

Its displacement magnitude is $\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5\,\mathrm{m}$, while its path length is $3+4=7\,\mathrm{m}$. Both values are correct; they answer different questions.

If the drone now travels $3\,\mathrm{m}$ west, add $(-3,0)\,\mathrm{m}$. The final displacement becomes $(0,4)\,\mathrm{m}$. The sign encodes direction rather than “less distance.”

## Where it breaks

Adding magnitudes generally does not give the magnitude of a vector sum. It works only for vectors pointing in the same direction. The component length formula above assumes perpendicular unit axes; it cannot be copied unchanged into arbitrary coordinates.

The zero vector has no unique direction, so normalizing it would divide by zero. In physics, also distinguish displacement from position, and speed from [velocity](concept:velocity). These distinctions become essential when you draw a [free-body diagram](concept:free-body).
