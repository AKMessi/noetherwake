## Intuition

Uniform motion does not need a continuing push. Net force measures the change in motion, not motion itself. Zero net force allows any constant velocity in an inertial frame.

## Formalism

$$\sum\mathbf F=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\mathbf v=\text{constant}$$

Newton first law defines inertial frames: frames where force-free bodies maintain velocity. The second law preserves this condition when net force vanishes.

[Explore velocity](concept:velocity). [Explore vectors](concept:vectors).

## Worked example

An object moves at 3 m/s with zero net force. Find velocity after 4 s.

Start with the defining relationship. Newton first law defines inertial frames: frames where force-free bodies maintain velocity. The second law preserves this condition when net force vanishes. Substitute the given values: **`3` = 3 m/s**. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

Accelerating frames need additional inertial terms. Zero velocity at an instant does not establish zero force.

A common trap is: **a moving object must have a forward net force**. Imagine friction removed: constant velocity has no changing momentum to explain.
