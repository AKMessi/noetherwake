## Intuition

A differential equation specifies how a state changes rather than listing every future state. An initial condition chooses one trajectory from the family allowed by the law.

## Formalism

$$
\dot y=ky,\qquad y(0)=y_0\quad\Rightarrow\quad y=y_0e^{kt}
$$

The exponential has exactly the required proportional rate; the initial value fixes its multiplier. Verify both the rate equation and the starting value.

[Explore exponential](concept:exponential). [Explore integral](concept:integral).

## Worked example

For y prime=2y and y(0)=3, find y prime(0).

Start with the defining relationship. The exponential has exactly the required proportional rate; the initial value fixes its multiplier. Verify both the rate equation and the starting value. Substitute the given values: **`2*3` = 6 **. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

A rate law without an initial condition usually does not determine a unique solution. More complex laws may need numerical integration.

A common trap is: **ignoring the initial condition**. Substitute t=0 into the proposed trajectory as a separate check.
