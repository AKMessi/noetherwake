## Intuition

The work needed to change speed grows with speed squared. This is why doubling speed requires four times the kinetic energy and makes stopping harder.

## Formalism

$$
K=\tfrac12mv^2,\qquad W_{net}=\Delta K
$$

Use F=m dv/dt and dx=v dt in work. The integral becomes the integral of mv dv, giving one-half mv squared at the endpoints.

[Explore work](concept:work). [Explore chain rule](concept:chain-rule).

## Worked example

Mass is 3 kg and speed 4 m/s. Find kinetic energy.

Start with the defining relationship. Use F=m dv/dt and dx=v dt in work. The integral becomes the integral of mv dv, giving one-half mv squared at the endpoints. Substitute the given values: **`0.5*3*4^2` = 24 J**. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

Kinetic energy depends on reference frame and is a scalar. Relativistic speeds require a different expression.

A common trap is: **doubling speed doubles kinetic energy**. Compare the square of twice the speed with the original square.
