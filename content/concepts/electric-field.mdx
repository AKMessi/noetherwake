## Intuition

An electric field describes what force a small positive test charge would feel at each point. It separates the source arrangement from the probe used to measure it.

## Formalism

$$
\mathbf E=\mathbf F/q_{test},\qquad\mathbf F=q\mathbf E
$$

Divide the Coulomb force on a test charge by that charge. The probe factor cancels, leaving a property of the sources and the position.

[Explore coulomb](concept:coulomb). [Explore newton 2](concept:newton-2). [Explore vectors](concept:vectors).

## Worked example

Force on a positive 3 C probe is 4 N right. Find signed field.

Start with the defining relationship. Divide the Coulomb force on a test charge by that charge. The probe factor cancels, leaving a property of the sources and the position. Substitute the given values: **`4/3` = 1.3333 N/C**. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

The test charge must not significantly rearrange the source. A negative probe feels force opposite the field.

A common trap is: **field direction follows a negative probe force**. Define direction with a positive test charge, then apply the sign of the actual charge.
