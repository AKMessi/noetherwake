## Intuition

An electric field describes what a small positive test charge would experience at a location. It turns “this charge pushes that charge” into a spatial map: a source creates a field, and a charge placed in that field feels a force.

The field exists as a description of the source even before a test charge is inserted. Its direction is defined using a positive test charge. A negative charge feels a force in the opposite direction.

## Formalism

For a sufficiently small test charge $q$, define

$$\mathbf{E}=\frac{\mathbf{F}}{q},\qquad\mathbf{F}=q\mathbf{E}.$$

The field unit is newtons per coulomb, equivalently volts per metre. For an isolated point source charge $Q$ in vacuum,

$$\mathbf{E}(r)=k\frac{Q}{r^2}\hat{\mathbf{r}},\qquad r>0.$$

Here $\hat{\mathbf{r}}$ points away from the source. Positive $Q$ gives an outward field; negative $Q$ gives an inward field. Use [Coulomb’s law](concept:coulomb), [charge](concept:charge) and [vectors](concept:vectors) to connect the magnitude and direction.

## Worked example

At one location, a uniform field points right with magnitude $200\,\mathrm{N/C}$. A particle of charge $-3\,\mathrm{\mu C}$ is placed there. What force acts on it?

Convert the charge first: $-3\,\mathrm{\mu C}=-3\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{C}$. With right as positive,

$$F_x=qE_x=(-3\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{C})(200\,\mathrm{N/C})=-6\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{N}.$$

The force has magnitude $0.0006\,\mathrm{N}$ and points left. Coulombs cancel, and the negative sign carries direction. Changing the particle’s charge changes the force, not the externally specified field.

## Where it breaks

A large test charge can rearrange the sources, changing the field you were trying to measure. The test-charge definition assumes its disturbance is negligible. The point-source formula is also singular at $r=0$ and does not describe the interior structure of a real charged object.

Field lines are a drawing convention, not physical tracks that charges must follow. A moving particle’s trajectory also depends on its initial velocity. With multiple sources, add the field vectors using [superposition](concept:superposition); do not simply add their magnitudes.
