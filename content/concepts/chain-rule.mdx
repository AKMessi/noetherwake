## Intuition

If one quantity changes because another changed, the total rate multiplies the two local conversion rates. Metres per turn times turns per second gives metres per second.

## Formalism

$$\frac{d}{dx}f(g(x))=f\prime(g(x))g\prime(x)$$

The inner change is approximately g prime times the input change. The outer change is f prime times that inner change. Divide by the original input change.

[Explore composition](concept:composition). [Explore derivative](concept:derivative).

## Worked example

For f(x)=(3x+4)^2, find f prime at x=0.

Start with the defining relationship. The inner change is approximately g prime times the input change. The outer change is f prime times that inner change. Divide by the original input change. Substitute the given values: **`2*3*4` = 24 **. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

Both derivatives are evaluated at their own inputs. Omitting the inner rate gives the wrong units and the wrong scale.

A common trap is: **differentiating only the outside**. Name the intermediate variable u, then write output per u times u per x.
