## Intuition

A tangent line is the best first-order prediction near a known point. It keeps the known value and extends with the instantaneous slope.

## Formalism

$$
f(a+h)\approx f(a)+f\prime(a)h
$$

The derivative definition makes the error smaller than the input change as that change tends to zero. The line captures what survives at first order.

[Explore derivative](concept:derivative). [Explore linear functions](concept:linear-functions).

## Worked example

Use the tangent to x^2 at x=3 to estimate f(3+0.1).

Start with the defining relationship. The derivative definition makes the error smaller than the input change as that change tends to zero. The line captures what survives at first order. Substitute the given values: **`3^2+0.2*3` = 9.6 **. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

Approximation error grows with distance and curvature. A tangent is local information, not an exact global replacement.

A common trap is: **using the slope without anchoring the line**. Start the tangent at (a,f(a)); only then add slope times displacement.
