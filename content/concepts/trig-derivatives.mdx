## Intuition

A point moving around a unit circle has a tangent velocity perpendicular to its radius. Its vertical coordinate is sine; the vertical component of the tangent is cosine.

## Formalism

$$(\sin x)\prime=\cos x,\qquad(\cos x)\prime=-\sin x$$

A small radian angle sweeps an arc nearly equal to its chord. The tangent direction turns the radius by a quarter turn, giving the component rates.

## Worked example

For f(x)=sin(3x), find f prime at x=0.

Substitute the given values: **`3` = 3 **. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

These rates use radians. Degrees introduce an extra pi/180 scale factor.

A common trap is: **forgetting the minus sign for cosine**. At a small positive angle cosine is decreasing, so its derivative must be negative.
