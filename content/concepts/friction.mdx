## Intuition

Static friction supplies only the force needed to prevent relative slipping, up to a limit. Once surfaces slide, kinetic friction is often approximated by a fixed coefficient times normal force.

## Formalism

$$|f_s|\le\mu_sN,\qquad |f_k|=\mu_kN$$

Use horizontal force balance to find required static friction, then compare it with its maximum. Use the kinetic model only after slipping is established.

[Explore free body](concept:free-body). [Explore inequalities](concept:inequalities).

## Worked example

A surface has N=3*10 N and kinetic coefficient 0.2. Find kinetic friction magnitude.

Start with the defining relationship. Use horizontal force balance to find required static friction, then compare it with its maximum. Use the kinetic model only after slipping is established. Substitute the given values: **`2*3` = 6 N**. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

The coefficients are empirical approximations; friction can depend on speed, surface condition and temperature.

A common trap is: **static friction always equals its maximum**. Compute the force required for no slip before comparing against the ceiling.
