## Intuition

Constant acceleration adds equal amounts of velocity in equal times. The velocity-time graph is a line, so displacement is the area of a rectangle plus a triangle.

## Formalism

$$v=v_0+at,\qquad x=x_0+v_0t+\tfrac12at^2$$

Integrate the constant rate once to get velocity. Integrate that linear velocity again to get position, retaining both initial conditions.

[Explore acceleration](concept:acceleration). [Explore integral](concept:integral).

## Worked example

From rest, acceleration is 3 m/s^2 for 4 s. Find final velocity.

Start with the defining relationship. Integrate the constant rate once to get velocity. Integrate that linear velocity again to get position, retaining both initial conditions. Substitute the given values: **`3*4` = 12 m/s**. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

These formulas assume a is constant over the whole interval. They cannot be applied unchanged to drag or a spring.

A common trap is: **forgetting the one-half in displacement**. The added velocity builds from zero; its average over the interval is half its final value.
