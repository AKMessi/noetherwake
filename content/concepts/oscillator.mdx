## Intuition

A restoring force points back toward equilibrium. In an ideal spring it grows in proportion to displacement, producing an acceleration that opposes position and repeats in time.

## Formalism

$$
m\ddot x=-kx,\quad\omega=\sqrt{k/m},\quad T=2\pi\sqrt{m/k}
$$

Apply Newton second law to Hooke force. A cosine has second derivative minus its frequency squared times itself; matching coefficients fixes the frequency.

## Worked example

Spring stiffness is 3 N/m and mass 4 kg. Find angular frequency.

Start with the defining relationship. Apply Newton second law to Hooke force. A cosine has second derivative minus its frequency squared times itself; matching coefficients fixes the frequency. Substitute the given values: **`sqrt(3/4)` = 0.86603 rad/s**. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

Hooke law is a small-deformation approximation. Damping changes amplitude and a driving force can supply energy.

A common trap is: **larger amplitude always means a longer period**. For the linear equation, amplitude multiplies both sides and cancels from the frequency condition.
