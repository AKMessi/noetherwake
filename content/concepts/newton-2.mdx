## Intuition

Force changes momentum. For a constant mass, it changes velocity at a rate proportional to the net force and inversely proportional to the mass. The vector sum matters, not any single push.

## Formalism

$$
\sum\mathbf F=m\mathbf a
$$

Begin with net force as momentum rate: F=dp/dt. For constant mass p=mv, differentiation gives m dv/dt, so F=ma.

[Explore newton 1](concept:newton-1). [Explore acceleration](concept:acceleration). [Explore equations](concept:equations).

## Worked example

A net force of 3 N acts on mass 4 kg. Find acceleration.

Start with the defining relationship. Begin with net force as momentum rate: F=dp/dt. For constant mass p=mv, differentiation gives m dv/dt, so F=ma. Substitute the given values: **`3/4` = 0.75 m/s^2**. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

Use the total external force and constant mass. Variable-mass systems require a momentum-flow account rather than blindly using F=ma.

A common trap is: **using the largest force instead of the net force**. Add signed force components first, then divide by the mass.
