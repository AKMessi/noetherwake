## Intuition

A signed accumulation cancels portions below the axis against portions above it. Physical totals such as distance need nonnegative contributions, so the sign must be handled before adding.

## Formalism

$$
\text{area}=\int_a^b |f(x)|\,dx
$$

Split at zero crossings, reverse the sign of negative portions, and add the positive pieces. Between two graphs use upper minus lower.

[Explore integral](concept:integral). [Explore functions](concept:functions).

## Worked example

Find the area under y=3 from x=0 to x=4.

Start with the defining relationship. Split at zero crossings, reverse the sign of negative portions, and add the positive pieces. Between two graphs use upper minus lower. Substitute the given values: **`3*4` = 12 **. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

Integrating before locating crossings can give cancellation instead of total area.

A common trap is: **calling every signed integral an area**. Shade the negative part and decide whether the question asks net change or total magnitude.
