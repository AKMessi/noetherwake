## Intuition

Impulse accumulates force over time and gives the resulting momentum change. Spreading a required momentum change over longer time reduces the average force.

## Formalism

$$
\mathbf J=\int\mathbf F\,dt=\Delta\mathbf p
$$

Integrate the momentum-rate form of Newton second law. The left side sums force-time strips; the right side telescopes to final minus initial momentum.

[Explore momentum](concept:momentum). [Explore integral](concept:integral).

## Worked example

A constant 3 N force acts for 4 s. Find impulse.

Start with the defining relationship. Integrate the momentum-rate form of Newton second law. The left side sums force-time strips; the right side telescopes to final minus initial momentum. Substitute the given values: **`3*4` = 12 N*s**. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

Peak force can differ greatly from average force. A short collision still requires checking external impulse if the system is not isolated.

A common trap is: **confusing impulse with force**. Write force times time and compare the units with momentum.
