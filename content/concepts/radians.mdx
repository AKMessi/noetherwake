## Intuition

An angle compares travelled arc length with radius. Measuring it this way makes a full turn the same number for every circle, and removes conversion factors from calculus.

## Formalism

$$
\theta=s/r,\qquad 2\pi\text{ radians per turn}
$$

Doubling the radius doubles the arc for the same opening. Their ratio depends only on the angle.

[Explore ratios](concept:ratios). [Explore coordinates](concept:coordinates).

## Worked example

An arc is 3 m long on radius 4 m. Find the angle in radians.

Start with the defining relationship. Doubling the radius doubles the arc for the same opening. Their ratio depends only on the angle. Substitute the given values: **`3/4` = 0.75 rad**. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

Calculus trigonometric rules assume radians. Degree inputs need a factor of pi/180.

A common trap is: **using degrees inside derivative formulas**. Convert to arc length divided by radius before applying a calculus identity.
