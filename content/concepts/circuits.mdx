## Intuition

Circuit equations join charge conservation at junctions with energy conservation around loops. Components determine how a shared current or voltage divides.

## Formalism

$$R_{series}=\sum R_i,\qquad\frac1{R_{parallel}}=\sum\frac1{R_i}$$

Series elements share current, so their voltage drops add to I times the sum of resistances. Parallel elements share voltage, so their currents add to V times the sum of conductances.

[Explore resistance](concept:resistance). [Explore systems](concept:systems). [Explore conservation energy](concept:conservation-energy).

## Worked example

Find series resistance of 3 ohm and 4 ohm.

Start with the defining relationship. Series elements share current, so their voltage drops add to I times the sum of resistances. Parallel elements share voltage, so their currents add to V times the sum of conductances. Substitute the given values: **`3+4` = 7 ohm**. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

Lumped steady-circuit rules assume negligible propagation delay and omitted induction. Real batteries have internal resistance.

A common trap is: **adding parallel resistances directly**. Parallel gives an extra route for charge, so equivalent resistance must be smaller than either positive branch.
