## Intuition

Composition feeds the output of one process into another. Order matters: warming water then measuring expansion is a different sequence from scaling a temperature reading first.

## Formalism

$$(f\circ g)(x)=f(g(x))$$

Evaluate the inner function, then treat that result as the outer function input. Keep parentheses around the whole inner expression.

[Explore functions](concept:functions).

## Worked example

f(x)=x^2, g(x)=x+3. Find f(g(4)).

Start with the defining relationship. Evaluate the inner function, then treat that result as the outer function input. Keep parentheses around the whole inner expression. Substitute the given values: **`(3+4)^2` = 49 **. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

The output of the inner rule must lie in the outer domain. Composition is generally not commutative.

A common trap is: **replacing composition with multiplication**. Draw two machines in sequence and label the intermediate value.
