## Intuition

A rotating radius has horizontal and vertical shadows. Cosine and sine are those shadows divided by radius. The unit circle extends right-triangle ratios to every angle.

## Formalism

$$
x=r\cos\theta,\quad y=r\sin\theta,\quad \sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1
$$

Pythagoras applied to the two projections gives the identity. Signs follow the quadrant, not a memorized triangle.

[Explore radians](concept:radians). [Explore coordinates](concept:coordinates).

## Worked example

A radius is 3 m. Find its vertical projection at pi/2 radians.

Start with the defining relationship. Pythagoras applied to the two projections gives the identity. Signs follow the quadrant, not a memorized triangle. Substitute the given values: **`3` = 3 m**. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

Tangent is undefined when cosine is zero. Inverse trig functions return principal branches, not every possible angle.

A common trap is: **swapping the adjacent and opposite sides**. Draw the angle against the horizontal axis and trace its horizontal shadow.
