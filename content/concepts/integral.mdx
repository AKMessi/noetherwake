## Intuition

An integral accumulates many tiny contributions. A velocity-time strip has height velocity and width time, so its area is a small displacement. Refining the strips captures changing rates.

## Formalism

$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum f(x_i)\Delta x$$

Partition the input interval, multiply each local rate by its small width, and add. Positive and negative contributions keep their signs.

[Explore limits](concept:limits). [Explore power rule](concept:power-rule).

## Worked example

A constant rate is 3 per second for 4 seconds. Find its accumulated total.

Start with the defining relationship. Partition the input interval, multiply each local rate by its small width, and add. Positive and negative contributions keep their signs. Substitute the given values: **`3*4` = 12 **. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

A signed integral is not always geometric area. Convergence needs checking for unbounded functions or intervals.

A common trap is: **dropping the width of a strip**. Check dimensions: a rate must be multiplied by an input interval before it becomes a total.
