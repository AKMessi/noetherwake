## Intuition

A fraction counts equal pieces of a specified whole. Three quarters of a metre and three quarters of a minute share arithmetic, but describe different things. The denominator names the size of the piece; the numerator counts pieces.

## Formalism

$$\frac a b+\frac c b=\frac{a+c}{b}$$

Cut every whole into the same number of pieces before adding. Doubling both the piece count and pieces per whole leaves the amount unchanged.



## Worked example

A ribbon is 3/4 metres long. You add 4/4 metres. What is the total in metres?

Start with the defining relationship. Cut every whole into the same number of pieces before adding. Doubling both the piece count and pieces per whole leaves the amount unchanged. Substitute the given values: **`(3+4)/4` = 1.75 m**. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

A denominator cannot be zero. Adding denominators changes the size of the pieces and is not addition of quantities.

A common trap is: **adding denominators**. Rename both fractions with one common denominator, then add only the numerators.
