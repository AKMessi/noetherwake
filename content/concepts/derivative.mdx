## Intuition

Zoom into a smooth curve until it resembles a straight line. The derivative is the slope of that local line: output change per tiny input change. For position, that rate is velocity.

## Formalism

$$f\prime(x)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}h$$

For x squared, the secant quotient expands to 2x+h. As the interval shrinks, the extra h vanishes and leaves 2x.

[Explore limits](concept:limits). [Explore slope](concept:slope).

## Worked example

For f(x)=x^2, find the derivative at x=3.

Start with the defining relationship. For x squared, the secant quotient expands to 2x+h. As the interval shrinks, the extra h vanishes and leaves 2x. Substitute the given values: **`2*3` = 6 **. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

A corner or jump can destroy the limit. A derivative is a rate at a point, not the total change across a wide interval.

A common trap is: **using f(x)/x as the derivative**. Compare two nearby outputs and divide their difference by the input difference.
