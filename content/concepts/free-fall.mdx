## Intuition

Without air resistance, near-Earth gravity accelerates all freely falling objects equally. Mass increases both gravitational force and resistance to acceleration, so it cancels.

## Formalism

$$
ma=-mg\quad\Rightarrow\quad a=-g
$$

Choose upward positive. Gravitational force points downward, so Newton second law gives negative acceleration. Integrate that constant rate for velocity and height.

[Explore constant acceleration](concept:constant-acceleration). [Explore newton 2](concept:newton-2).

## Worked example

Use g=10 m/s^2. Drop from rest for 3 s. Find downward distance.

Start with the defining relationship. Choose upward positive. Gravitational force points downward, so Newton second law gives negative acceleration. Integrate that constant rate for velocity and height. Substitute the given values: **`5*3^2` = 45 m**. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

Near-surface constant g and negligible air resistance are approximations. Different drag-to-mass ratios break the equal-fall prediction.

A common trap is: **heavier objects fall faster in vacuum**. Write both the force mg and inertial factor m, then cancel the same nonzero mass.
