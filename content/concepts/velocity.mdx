## Intuition

Velocity tells how quickly location changes and in which direction. The slope of a position-time graph is velocity; a curved position graph therefore means changing velocity.

## Formalism

$$v=\frac{dx}{dt},\qquad\Delta x=\int v\,dt$$

Average velocity is displacement divided by elapsed time. Shrinking the interval produces the position derivative, linking motion to local slope.

[Explore position](concept:position). [Explore derivative](concept:derivative).

## Worked example

For x(t)=3t^2 m, find v at t=4 s.

Start with the defining relationship. Average velocity is displacement divided by elapsed time. Shrinking the interval produces the position derivative, linking motion to local slope. Substitute the given values: **`2*3*4` = 24 m/s**. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

Speed is the magnitude of velocity. Zero average velocity does not mean the object stayed still.

A common trap is: **reading graph height as velocity**. Use a small right triangle on the position graph and compute its slope.
