## Intuition

Even at constant speed, a circular trajectory changes velocity because direction turns. The required acceleration points inward, toward the centre of curvature.

## Formalism

$$
a_c=v^2/r,\qquad F_{radial}=mv^2/r
$$

Two nearby velocity arrows differ by the same small angle as their radius arrows. Their difference has magnitude v times the small angle; dividing by time gives v squared over radius.

[Explore trig](concept:trig). [Explore acceleration](concept:acceleration). [Explore newton 2](concept:newton-2).

## Worked example

Speed is 3 m/s on radius 4 m. Find inward acceleration.

Start with the defining relationship. Two nearby velocity arrows differ by the same small angle as their radius arrows. Their difference has magnitude v times the small angle; dividing by time gives v squared over radius. Substitute the given values: **`3^2/4` = 2.25 m/s^2**. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

Centripetal describes the net radial force, not an extra force to add. Variable speed can also require tangential acceleration.

A common trap is: **adding a separate centripetal force to tension or gravity**. Identify which real interaction supplies the inward resultant.
