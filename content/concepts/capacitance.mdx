## Intuition

A capacitor stores separated charge, and therefore field energy. Adding charge becomes harder as the voltage rises; capacitance measures how much charge is stored per volt.

## Formalism

$$
Q=CV,\qquad U=\tfrac12CV^2
$$

Move a small charge dq when the voltage is q/C. The incremental work is (q/C)dq. Integrate from zero to Q to obtain Q squared over 2C.

[Explore voltage](concept:voltage). [Explore integral](concept:integral). [Explore electric field](concept:electric-field).

## Worked example

Capacitance is 3 F and voltage 4 V. Find stored charge.

Start with the defining relationship. Move a small charge dq when the voltage is q/C. The incremental work is (q/C)dq. Integrate from zero to Q to obtain Q squared over 2C. Substitute the given values: **`3*4` = 12 C**. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

The constant-capacitance model neglects dielectric nonlinearities, leakage and breakdown. Large voltages can invalidate it physically.

A common trap is: **using qv instead of one-half qv for charging energy**. The voltage rises from zero during charging; its average is half the final value for constant C.
