## Intuition

The dot product measures how much one vector points along another. A perpendicular push contributes no displacement-direction work, even if the force is large.

## Formalism

$$
\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b=a_xb_x+a_yb_y=|a||b|\cos\theta
$$

Project one vector onto the direction of the other and multiply by its length. Component products encode that same aligned part.

[Explore vectors](concept:vectors).

## Worked example

Find (3,2) dot (4,3).

Start with the defining relationship. Project one vector onto the direction of the other and multiply by its length. Component products encode that same aligned part. Substitute the given values: **`3*4+6` = 18 **. Keep the unit through the calculation and check whether the sign and scale fit the situation.

## Where it breaks

The dot product is a scalar. It cannot measure the turning direction that a cross product captures.

A common trap is: **using a sine for aligned work**. Parallel vectors must have maximal positive dot product; test the angle-zero case.
